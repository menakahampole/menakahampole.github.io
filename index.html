Menaka Hampole

Assistant Professor of Finance · Yale School of Management

Welcome! I am an Assistant Professor of Finance at the Yale School of Management. My research lies at the intersection of household finance and labor economics, with a focus on education financing, gender gaps in labor and financial markets, and the economic consequences of technological change.

I received my PhD in Finance from the Kellogg School of Management, Northwestern University (2023).

Working Papers

  1. 1.
    Peer Effects and the Gender Gap in Corporate Leadership: Evidence from MBA Students
    with Francesca Truffa and Ashley Wong
    Conditionally accepted, Quarterly Journal of Economics
    WFA Best Empirical Finance Paper 2025 Wharton-WRDS Best Empirical Finance Paper 2025 Carlo Dell'Aringa Young Economist Prize 2023 Outstanding Paper — Discrimination & Diversity Workshop 2022
    PDF SSRN
    Using administrative data from top U.S. business schools linked to publicly available LinkedIn profiles, we study how the gender composition of MBA cohorts shapes career outcomes. We find that women are more likely to enter senior management positions when they attend business school alongside a larger share of female peers.
  2. 2.
    Artificial Intelligence and the Labor Market
    with Dimitris Papanikolaou, Lawrence Schmidt, and Bryan Seegmiller
    NBER Working Paper No. 33509 (2025)
    NBER SSRN
    We use natural language processing to develop novel measures of workers' task-level exposure to AI and machine learning technologies from 2010 to 2023.
  3. 3.
    Does Distance Still Matter? Evidence from 30 Years of Mortgage Lending
    with Adam Jørring and Joao Monteiro
    2025
    SSRN
    We document that geographic distance from bank branches remains associated with lower credit quality and higher pricing in mortgage markets. These patterns have persisted over 30 years despite the growth of securitization and online banking.
  4. 4.
    Universal No-Loan Policies
    Menaka Hampole
    PDF
    I study 22 U.S. universities that replaced student loans with grants through universal no-loan policies (UNLPs) between 2001 and 2021. I find that UNLPs increased the share of students choosing high-paying majors by 6%.
